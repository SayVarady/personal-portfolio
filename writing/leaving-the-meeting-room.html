Leaving the Meeting Room, Reclaiming the Editor

Mistake Decision

Three years at an HR SaaS startup took me from writing features to sitting in product meetings, running technical calls, and leading a team through implementation problems I used to solve myself. On paper, that's progress. Most weeks, it felt like it.

The Mistake

The mistake wasn't taking on more responsibility — it was not noticing what it was quietly costing. I kept telling myself the itch to write code again was just nostalgia, something senior engineers grow out of. So I ignored it, month after month, and back-filled the feeling with "leading" instead of "building."

It took longer than it should have to admit that the best part of most weeks was the one afternoon I still got to touch a codebase directly — not because management is lesser work, but because it wasn't the work I actually wanted more of.

The Decision

Once I named it honestly, the decision was less dramatic than I expected: move back toward backend and full-stack engineering on purpose, instead of treating it as something I'd get to "eventually." Sharpen the technical edge that management had quietly been pulling me away from.

That's the actual reason this site exists in its current form — not a portfolio to prove I can manage people, but one built to show I can still design the system, write the code, and solve the problem myself. And to open the door to working with development teams beyond Cambodia, where that's the work on offer.

The role I was good at and the role I wanted turned out to be two different jobs. Noticing that late is the mistake. Acting on it late is optional.
